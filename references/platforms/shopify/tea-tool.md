# Tea-Tool / Tea Ware Shopify Image Rules

When the task involves tea tools and tea ware sold on a Shopify premium DTC store — Yixing zisha teapots, gaiwans, tea cups, fair cups, tea trays, tea pets, 工夫茶具套装, electric tea kettles, gongfu tea sets, tea gift boxes, travel tea sets, tea accessories — default to this logic. The visual reference is **Aesop × Wildness × 观夏 × 野兽派 × Yixing heritage**: museum-grade still life, dramatic warm light, ritual, and craft. Not Wayfair marketplace thumbnails. Not Taobao red-banner posters.

## Applicable Categories

- **Yixing & Purple Clay**: Zisha teapots (西施/石瓢/仿古/井栏/龙蛋/潘壶), gaiwans, tea cups, fair cups (公道杯), tea strainers, tea pets (茶宠)
- **Porcelain & Ceramic**: Jingdezhen porcelain sets, Ru / Jun / Ge / Ding ware, celadon, tea cups, saucers, master cups
- **Glass**: Borosilicate glass fair cups, glass teapots, glass tea sets, heat-resistant glass kettles
- **Wood & Bamboo**: Tea trays (乌金石/电木/黑檀/胡桃木/竹), tea board, tea mat, 茶道六君子
- **Metal**: Silver / tin / copper / brass teapots, copper kettles (铜壶), silver tea sets, 925 silver tea accessories
- **Gongfu Tea Sets (工夫茶具)**: Pot + fair cup + 4-6 cups + tray + cloth, packaged as gift
- **Modern Tea Ware**: Electric tea kettles, automatic water-filling kettles, smart tea masters, 煮茶器, 茶艺炉
- **Tea Gift Boxes**: Gift sets for festivals, business gifting, wedding, Mid-Autumn, Spring Festival
- **Travel & Outdoor Tea**: Travel tea sets, portable gongfu sets, outdoor tea ware
- **Tea Accessories**: Tea pets, 茶道六君子 (tea tongs/scoop/needle/funnel/clip), tea towels, tea canister, tea caddies

## The Core Philosophy

Tea-tool images fail in three ways: (1) the white-background catalog shot that erases all the ritual and craft, (2) the busy red-gold Taobao poster that screams "sale," and (3) the over-styled "茶道表演" photo that looks like a documentary, not a luxury product. All three are wrong for a Shopify premium DTC store.

**The tea-tool Shopify image hierarchy is: Ritual → Material → Craft → Form → Detail.**

The viewer must feel the **ritual of tea** (the act of pouring, the warmth of clay, the stillness of a tea table) before they appreciate the **material** (zisha grain, porcelain glaze, wood patina), then the **craft** (hand-pulled spouts, kiln-fired glaze, hand-carved decoration), and finally the **form** (the silhouette, the proportion, the spout arc). A teapot without ritual is just a clay object. A teapot with ritual is heritage.

**Product is the protagonist. The tea table is its stage. The light is the story.**

## Color Palette Rules

Tea-tool palettes are **material-driven, not brand-driven** — but premium DTC sets share a moody, warm, low-key family:

- **Yixing Zisha / Purple Clay (default for this category)**: Deep rosewood background `#3D1F0F` to deep red-brown `#4A1810`. Teapot body in zisha red-brown `#6B3A1A` to auburn `#7A4A2A`. White porcelain accessories in warm cream `#F5F0E8`. Tea liquor in deep amber `#A8551A` to burnt sienna `#8B4513`. Warm directional light at 2700K-3000K. Deep shadows. This is the gold standard for Yixing DTC.
- **Light Porcelain / Jingdezhen (Jade White)**: Warm off-white `#F8F4ED` background. Porcelain in cool ivory `#EFE8DA` to soft celadon `#A8B89A`. Black tea liquor. Subtle blue-grey shadows. Diffused northern light. Quieter, more refined than zisha.
- **Celadon / Ru Ware (Song Dynasty Aesthetic)**: Cool blue-grey background `#5A6A6E` to soft jade `#A8B89A`. Celadon glaze in misty green-blue. Aged patina. Soft, contemplative light. For high-end celadon or Ru ware reproductions.
- **Modern Kettle / Electric Tea Master (Tech-Craft)**: Deep charcoal `#1A1A22` to warm black `#0A0A0F`. Brushed metal / chrome / copper accent. Warm 2700K LED ring glow. Single warm orange `#E89456` or amber `#D4A574` accent. Less ritual, more spec — for modern 煮茶器 / 茶艺炉.
- **Gift Box / Premium Set (Festive-Craft)**: Deep oxblood `#5C1A0F` + gold leaf `#C9A968` + warm cream `#F5F0E8`. Slightly more saturated than the pure ritual palette — but still restrained, no red-banner energy.

**Universal rule**: 2-3 main colors + 1 product color + 1 neutral. Background is always a member of the rosewood / charcoal / celadon / oxblood family — never pure white, never pure black, never bright red. Light is always warm directional (2700K-3000K), never cold studio flash.

## The Light is the Brand

Light is more important than product in tea-tool imagery. It tells the story.

- **2700K-3000K warm directional light**, from top-left or top-right. Single source preferred.
- **Deep shadows**. The zisha teapot should sit half in shadow, half in light. Shadow reveals form.
- **Rim light** on the spout, handle, and lid knob — separates the silhouette from the dark background.
- **Specular highlight** on glazed porcelain — a small, controlled bright spot showing the glaze quality.
- **Tea liquor glow**: when tea is poured, the amber stream should catch light and read luminous.
- **No flat front-flash. No over-bright HDR. No cold studio light.** Cold light kills the ritual mood instantly.

## Default Main Image Logic

Main image recommended **1:1**, for search results, collection pages, product card primary.

### Visual Structure

- **Product hero at 55%-65% of frame.** Lower than 3C (where the device dominates) but higher than furniture (where the room dominates). The teapot is the protagonist; the tea table is its stage.
- **Background**: deep rosewood wood texture, or soft dark gradient, or controlled dark studio. Never pure white. Never random lifestyle clutter.
- **Supporting element**: white porcelain saucer, white cup with amber tea, or bare rosewood table. Maximum 1-2 supporting elements.
- **Lighting**: warm 2700K directional from top-left. Deep shadow on the right side of the teapot. Rim light on spout and handle. Specular highlight on the lid.
- **Optional hand**: if including a hand, only the hand and wrist — never the face. The hand must feel natural, not posed. Fingers lightly gripping handle, or palm cradling base.
- **On-image text**: optional. If used, 1 line maximum — product name or material in small, refined Chinese serif (思源宋体 / 方正宋刻本悦宋) or English serif (Cormorant Garamond / Playfair Display). Placed low and subtle. Never center-aligned sales copy. Never red banner. Never price tag.

## Default Gallery Image Logic

Gallery recommended **4-6 images**, mixed ratios (1:1 + 3:4 for vertical portrait + 4:5 for editorial). Each image reveals a new layer of the tea ritual.

### Gallery 1: Alternate Angle / Spout Detail

**Goal**: Show the teapot from a second viewpoint, revealing the spout arc and lid fit.

- Same product, opposite side or 3/4 from a different angle.
- Reveals the spout's pour curve, the lid's fit, the handle's ergonomic shape.
- Same background and lighting as main image.
- **No text.**

### Gallery 2: Material Texture / Clay Grain

**Goal**: Make the viewer feel the clay.

- Extreme close-up of the zisha clay surface. Visible clay grain, the natural color variation, the subtle hand-finish lines.
- Shallow depth of field. Warm light raking across the surface to show texture.
- 1 short label acceptable: "原矿紫砂" / "Yixing Zisha" / "Hand-thrown Clay" / "朱泥" / "紫泥".

### Gallery 3: Decoration / Engraving Detail

**Goal**: Show the artwork on the teapot.

- Close-up of the carved / engraved / painted decoration — crane motif, bamboo, calligraphy, lotus, plum blossom.
- Reveals craft quality: depth of carving, line precision, glaze compatibility.
- Optional small label: decoration name in Chinese (松鹤延年 / 竹报平安 / 莲心).

### Gallery 4: In Ritual — Pouring / Holding

**Goal**: Show the teapot being used.

- Hand pouring — the amber stream falling into a white porcelain cup, the steam rising, the wrist tilted at 90° (the gongfu pour angle).
- Or: two hands cradling the teapot, fingers on lid, demonstrating the hold.
- The hand is supporting, not modeling. Forearm and wrist only. No face, no full body.
- This is the "ritual" image — it shows the product in its intended use.
- **No text** unless showing the pour angle as a craft feature.

### Gallery 5: Tea Set Composition

**Goal**: Show the product as part of a complete tea set.

- The teapot + fair cup (公道杯) + 4-6 small cups + tea tray, all in a quiet arrangement.
- 45° bird's-eye or low-angle side view. Same warm light, same rosewood background.
- Styling: minimalist. 1-2 supporting objects only (a tea towel, a single branch, a small teapot stand).
- This is the "set" image — useful for the gongfu tea set SKUs.
- **No text.**

### Gallery 6: Bottom Seal / Maker's Mark / 底款

**Goal**: Show the maker's mark — proof of authenticity and craft.

- Close-up of the bottom of the teapot, showing the carved seal (底款), the maker's name, the clay stamp.
- Soft, controlled light. The seal must be sharp and readable.
- This image is for serious tea collectors who verify the maker's mark.
- Optional small label: maker name in Chinese.

## Default PDP Logic

Tea-tool PDP is a **walk through a private tea moment**, not a feature list. Each screen is a moment in the ritual.

### 6-Screen PDP Structure (3:4 portrait)

1. **Hero Moment**: The main image repeated as full-bleed hero. Headline: 1 line about ritual or craft, not features. "一壶事茶，千年风骨" / "Hand-poured, slow-brewed, lived-with." Massive negative space. The product is the entire screen.
2. **Clay Origin / Material Story**: Where the clay comes from. Huanglongshan zisha mine, the firing temperature, the porosity, the aging. Macro texture of clay grain. 2-3 sentences of narrative copy. The message: this clay is special because of where it's from and how it breathes.
3. **Hand-Thrown / Craft**: The maker's hand, the wheel, the tools. Show the process — hand-pulled spout, hand-carved decoration, kiln loading. Or: extreme close-up of the join between handle and body, the lid fit, the spout's pour test. The message: a person made this, not a machine.
4. **In Ritual — The Pour**: A complete tea pour sequence. Two or three frames: hand lifting teapot, amber stream falling into cup, hand returning teapot to tray. Or one composed hero shot of the pour. Tea liquor catches light. This is the emotional peak of the PDP.
5. **Set Composition**: The full tea set arranged on a tray. 6 cups + fair cup + teapot + cloth. Wide shot, bird's-eye. One short line: "Set of {N} pieces" or "工夫茶器, 完整一套". This screen is for gongfu set SKUs.
6. **The Maker / Close**: The maker's portrait (if available) or the maker's seal close-up. The brand's craft story. Final line: "每一把壶，都是一次山与火的对话" / "Each pot, a conversation between mountain and fire." This is the signature close.

### PDP Coherence Rules (Tea-Tool Edition)

- **Same tea table across all screens.** If the hero is on a deep rosewood table, that table persists. The viewer is moving around the same ritual moment, not through different photo studios.
- **Same light quality.** 2700K warm directional from top-left in every screen. The spout highlight in the hero is the same color temperature as the rim light in the detail shots.
- **Same tea set, same angles.** The teapot, fair cup, cups, tray should be the same set across all screens. Don't mix zisha with porcelain cups in one screen and porcelain teapot in another. Consistency is luxury.
- **Typography is editorial, not commercial.** Tea-tool PDP typography should be editorial Chinese serif (思源宋体 / 方正宋刻本悦宋) or English serif (Cormorant Garamond / Garamond). Never display fonts. Never red. Never bold for emphasis — use weight contrast (300 / 600) and tracking instead.
- **Color rule**: 2-3 main colors per screen + product color + neutral. Never introduce a new color mid-set.

## Variant Image Logic

Tea-tool variants are clay and glaze stories:

- **2-3 images per clay/glaze variant**: **wide ritual shot** (teapot in context) + **material detail** (clay macro) + (optionally) **alternate angle**.
- Ritual shot must be identical in composition, lighting, and styling across variants — only the clay color/glaze changes. This lets the buyer compare without cognitive load.
- Material detail is unique to each variant — show the specific zisha clay color (朱泥 / 紫泥 / 段泥 / 老紫泥), or glaze color (青瓷 / 白瓷 / 钧瓷 / 哥窑).
- Clay/glaze name beneath each image: "朱泥 — 红艳温润" / "紫泥 — 古朴典雅" / "段泥 — 素雅米黄" / "青瓷 — 雨过天青".
- If the product is a tea set, variant images show different set configurations (6-cup set / 8-cup set / single pot + cup).

## Promotional Banner Logic

Tea-tool promo banners must never feel like a sale. The brand that screams "SALE" has already lost the premium tea buyer.

- Campaign theme should be seasonal or ritual-driven: "Tea Under the Moon" (Mid-Autumn), "Spring Tea Ceremony" (Spring), "Winter Warmer" (Winter), "Fathers of Craft" (about the makers), "A New Year of Tea" (Spring Festival). Never "Biggest Sale of the Year" as primary.
- If a price promotion exists, bury it — small type, tasteful placement, not the visual anchor.
- Banner layout: wide editorial (16:9) for hero banners, vertical (9:16) for Instagram / Pinterest. Product always in ritual context, never isolated.
- If using calligraphy, keep it hand-written quality, not printed red-banner.

## Compliance & Industry Red Lines (MUST READ)

These are non-negotiable. Failure on any one is a hard fail.

1. **Material claims must be honest.** If the user says "原矿紫砂" / "Yixing zisha," the image must show zisha grain texture. Do not label a generic clay teapot as "原矿黄龙山" / "Huanglongshan mine." Do not label modern machine-made as "全手工" / "fully hand-thrown." The label must match the actual product.
2. **No inflated certifications.** "国大师" / "National Master" / "省大师" / "省级非遗" / "省级工艺美术大师" — these titles are government-issued and verifiable. Do not assign a title that the user has not provided proof of. If the user has a maker's mark (底款), use only that, not a fabricated title.
3. **No invented maker stories.** "三代传承" / "百年老店" / "founded 1880" / "三百年制壶世家" — these are marketing claims that must come from the user. Do not write them into the image or copy unless the user provides verifiable history.
4. **No false health claims.** Tea is not medicine. Do not write "养生" / "治百病" / "延年益寿" / "抗衰老" / "排毒" / "降三高" / "抗癌" — these are medical claims and illegal in most markets. Allowed: "泡茶更香" / "激发茶香" / "饮茶好物" / "ritual of tea."
5. **No borrowed lineage or school.** Do not claim "千利休流" / "正山小种传承" / "唐宋御用" / "故宫授权" — these are real lineages or institutions and require authorization. Use generic phrases: "新中式" / "东方茶道" / "modern Chinese ritual" / "East Asian tea tradition."
6. **Capacity, dimensions, weight must match product.** If the teapot is 200ml, the image should not visually imply a 500ml size. The user-provided spec is the source of truth.
7. **No museum / temple / shrine location claims.** Do not place the teapot in or near real named heritage sites (故宫, 灵隐寺, 浅草寺, etc.) unless the user has shot there. Use generic "传统茶室" / "modern tea space" / "private tea room" — the place should be a mood, not a claim.
8. **No borrowed brand identity.** Do not copy the visual identity of 观夏, 野兽派, Aesop, Ippodo, MUJI, etc. The reference is for *mood and craft level*, not for layout, logo, color, or typography to be lifted.
9. **No platform UI in image.** No "Add to Cart," no price, no star rating, no "Free Shipping," no platform logo. The image is the product, the platform is Shopify.
10. **No real people's faces in ritual shots.** The hand and wrist are OK. The face is not — privacy and IP risk. Use anonymous, age-neutral hands.
